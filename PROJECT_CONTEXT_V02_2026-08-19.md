**# CHANGE LOG**



**## V02 — From Personality Prediction to Perspective Discovery**



**### Changed**



This version records a major change in how the purpose of the personality

test is understood.



The original objective was primarily:



> \*\*Use four questions to identify one of 60 possible personality positions.\*\*



The new understanding is:



> \*\*The test does not need to perfectly identify a person in order to be

> useful. It can succeed by making a person aware that different people

> naturally see the same situation differently.\*\*



This changes the test from a pure prediction mechanism into a possible

\*\*perspective-awareness tool\*\*.



\---



**### Why**



Early experiments did not always correctly identify a participant's

birth-year position.



At first this appeared to be a failure.



However, when two people answered the same question differently, something

unexpected happened:



They noticed that they naturally approached the same situation from

different perspectives.



This created curiosity.



Instead of:



> "My answer is correct."



the person may begin thinking:



> "Why did I choose this while the other person chose something completely

> different?"



This is considered potentially more valuable than an accurate prediction.



\---



**### What We Learned**



The test may be most valuable when it activates:



> \*\*Observation mode instead of decision mode.\*\*



Decision mode asks:



\- What is correct?

\- What should I do?

\- What should the other person do?

\- How do I defend my position?



Observation mode asks:



\- Why did this option attract me?

\- What did I notice?

\- Why did another person choose differently?

\- What might they see that I do not?



The goal is not to remove differences.



The goal is to make differences visible without creating contempt.



\---



**### New Central Principle**



> \*\*Do not ask which person sees the elephant correctly.  

> Ask what each person can see from where they stand.\*\*



And:



> \*\*The Council does not exist to find the person who is right.  

> It exists so that the elephant does not remain invisible.\*\*



\---



**# 1. THE ELEPHANT PRINCIPLE**



The central metaphor remains the elephant.



Imagine twelve people standing around a large elephant.



One sees a leg.



Another sees the trunk.



Another sees the tail.



Another sees an ear.



They may argue because each person believes their own observation describes

the whole animal.



But every observation may contain something real.



The problem is not necessarily falsehood.



The problem is incomplete perspective.



The Council of 60 is therefore conceived as a map of different positions

around the same metaphorical elephant.



No single position should be assumed to contain the whole picture.



\---



**# 2. COUNCIL OF 60**



The project uses the traditional structure of:



12 × 5 = 60



as the initial framework for exploring 60 possible perspectives.



The Chinese zodiac is the structural inspiration, but the project should

not be presented merely as Chinese astrology.



The deeper hypothesis is:



> \*\*Could the 60 traditional combinations represent a useful map of

> different ways of observing, interpreting and approaching reality?\*\*



This remains a hypothesis.



It is not currently established as scientific fact.



\---



**# 3. PERSONALITY IS NOT A FIXED BOX**



A person is influenced by:



\- upbringing

\- family

\- culture

\- education

\- social environment

\- relationships

\- historical period

\- life experience

\- success and failure

\- current circumstances



Therefore observed behaviour cannot automatically be treated as a pure

expression of an underlying archetype.



An important change in our thinking is that we no longer need to completely

"clean" these influences away.



Instead:



> \*\*Preserve the differences. Observe them.\*\*



The test may reveal the perspective that currently attracts a person rather

than some perfectly isolated "pure personality".



\---



**# 4. THE REAL PURPOSE OF THE TEST**



The original goal:



> Predict the person's position.



The broader goal:



> \*\*Create awareness of perspective.\*\*



A person does not need to believe the system.



They do not need to accept a label.



They do not even need to agree with the result.



The important moment may simply be:



> \*\*"Someone else looked at the same thing and saw something different."\*\*



That is the seed.



\---



**# 5. THE SEED OF PERSPECTIVE**



The test can plant a small question:



> \*\*"Why did I choose this?"\*\*



and then:



> \*\*"Why did they choose something else?"\*\*



This can move a person from:



> certainty → curiosity



and from:



> judgment → observation



The test should therefore be designed so that different answers are

legitimate.



\---



**# 6. DIFFERENCE WITHOUT CONTEMPT**



A critical design principle:



Every answer must be attractive to somebody.



No answer should be deliberately:



\- stupid

\- weak

\- ridiculous

\- cowardly

\- immoral

\- socially inferior



All options should have comparable intellectual and emotional value.



The participant should be able to think:



> "That is not my way, but I understand why someone else would choose it."



The desired result is:



> \*\*Difference without contempt.\*\*



\---



**# 7. THE CURRENT FOUR-LAYER TEST**



The current experimental architecture contains four levels:



1\. Yin / Yang

2\. Trigon

3\. Animal

4\. Element



Each layer currently uses a different type of question.



This should NOT be changed all at once.



We need controlled experiments so that changes can be evaluated separately.



\---



**# 8. LAYER 1 — YIN / YANG**



The current version is considered strong and should remain unchanged for now.



**### Yang**



> "Rather than thinking about things for a long time, it is better to start

> acting immediately, go directly to the point and assert one's will

> outwardly."



**### Yin**



> "Rather than acting blindly, it is better to withdraw into calm,

> quietly analyze the situation and wait for the right inner impulse."



The strength of this question is that both answers are legitimate.



It does not ask:



> "Are you active or passive?"



It presents two different strategies.



**### Status**



🟢 \*\*CURRENTLY STRONG — KEEP FOR NOW\*\*



\---



**# 9. LAYER 2 — TRIGON**



The current version uses four challenges.



The participant chooses the one that attracts them most.



The important principle is:



> \*\*Which challenge attracts you?\*\*



rather than:



> "Which description is most like you?"



The four challenges have so far produced relatively clear choices without

excessive hesitation.



This layer should therefore remain as a baseline.



It can be refined later, but should not yet be completely replaced.



**### Status**



🟢 \*\*WORKING WELL — REFINE, DO NOT REPLACE\*\*



\---



**# 10. LAYER 3 — ANIMAL**



The current version uses three temptations.



**## Temptation 1 — Secret**



> "I have an exclusive, strictly guarded secret and access to information

> nobody else knows. Come with me behind the curtain, but not a word to

> anyone."



**## Temptation 2 — Spotlight**



> "This is your chance to show yourself in full, attract everyone's

> attention and make sure the world never forgets how exceptional you are."



**## Temptation 3 — Challenge**



> "Everyone says it is impossible and nobody can do it. Are you ready to

> take the risk, break the limits and prove them wrong?"



These categories produced useful differentiation.



However, one problem appeared.



A single temptation may contain multiple psychological attractions.



For example, "spotlight" may mean:



\- dominance

\- prestige

\- recognition

\- artistic appreciation

\- social approval

\- desire to be understood



These are not necessarily the same thing.



Therefore a person may choose a temptation for a secondary reason.



\---



**# 11. THE GOAT / RABBIT RESULT**



One participant whose known position corresponded to Goat received a result

closer to Rabbit.



This should not simply be treated as a failed prediction.



It revealed an important possibility:



> \*\*A person's situational attraction may not always equal their deepest

> or most stable perspective.\*\*



It also showed that some formulations may contain multiple signals.



The lesson is not:



> "The model is wrong."



The lesson is:



> \*\*"The question may not yet be sufficiently clean."\*\*



**### Status**



🟡 \*\*HYPOTHESIS — NEEDS FURTHER TESTING\*\*



\---



**# 12. LAYER 4 — ELEMENT**



The current version presents five descriptive statements.



**### Wood — Growth and Development**



> "My life has meaning when I continually develop, create new things and

> see my ideas help people around me grow."



**### Fire — Passion and Intensity**



> "I need to feel strong emotions, burn passionately for what I do and

> dynamically change the world around me despite obstacles."



**### Earth — Stability and Results**



> "What matters most is building solid foundations, material security and

> knowing that I can rely on the results of my work in the long term."



**### Metal — Order and Justice**



> "I believe in clear rules, discipline and fairness; I can be uncompromising

> with myself and others in pursuit of truth and respect."



**### Water — Intuition and Communication**



> "I perceive the world through deep feelings and intuition; I can adapt to

> any situation and naturally flow toward where I need to go."



Participants may hesitate because these statements invite self-analysis.



A person may think:



> "I am partly this and partly that."



This is potentially different from the trigon layer, where participants often

responded more spontaneously.



**### Status**



🟡 \*\*NEEDS REDESIGN / EXPERIMENTATION\*\*

**# 13. A NEW IDEA — QUOTATIONS**



A major new direction emerged:



Instead of asking people to describe themselves, present them with two

valuable thoughts.



For example:



> \*\*"The strength of a person is not that they see everything.  

> It is that they can listen to those who see what they cannot."\*\*



versus another equally meaningful thought.



Then ask:



> \*\*"Which thought speaks to you more?"\*\*



The participant does not need to know what is being measured.



They simply choose the thought that attracts them more.



This may reduce conscious self-description and social desirability.



\---



**# 14. THE STRENGTH QUOTATION**



One particularly strong original formulation emerged:



> \*\*"The strength of a person is not that they see everything.  

> It is that they can listen to those who see what they cannot."\*\*



This should be preserved as an important project quotation.



It does not explicitly label the reader as a leader.



A leader may still recognize themselves in it.



It does not ask a person to become more like someone else.



It simply acknowledges the limits of individual perspective.



\---



**# 15. LEADERSHIP AND PERSPECTIVE**



The project should not attempt to convince a naturally dominant person:



> "You need to become more empathetic."



That would impose one preferred perspective on another.



A leader may instead be approached through their own strength.



The message becomes:



> \*\*"Your strength does not require you to see everything.

> Your strength can include listening to those who see what you cannot."\*\*



The leader does not stop leading.



They gain access to perspectives they could not see alone.



This is an example of a broader principle:



> \*\*Do not change the person. Expand the picture.\*\*



\---



**# 16. SPEAKING TO DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVES**



This led to another possible future function of the Council.



Once a person understands their own perspective and has some understanding

of another person's perspective, they may communicate differently.



Instead of:



> "Do this."



one might say:



> "I need help. I cannot solve this as well as you can, and I think you may

> have exactly the perspective I am missing. Could you help me?"



The objective is not manipulation.



It is to recognize the unique contribution that another perspective can make.



\---



\# 17. A POSSIBLE FUTURE FOUR-STEP COUNCIL



The Council may eventually guide people through four stages:



**### 1. Know your place.**



> Where am I standing around the elephant?



**### 2. Know their place.**



> Where might the other person be standing?



**### 3. Speak their language.**



> How can I communicate in a way that respects their perspective?



**### 4. Complete the elephant.**



> What can they see that I cannot?



This may eventually become more important than accurately predicting

a person's birth year.

**# 18. QUOTATIONS AS A TESTING TOOL**



There are many existing quotations that could provide valuable material.



However, quotations must be verified.



For every historical quotation we should ideally record:



\- exact wording

\- author

\- original source

\- date if known

\- attribution confidence

\- author birth information if available

\- subject

\- theme

\- implied value

\- possible perspective



Famous quotations are often incorrectly attributed.



The project should never assign a quotation to a historical person merely

because the internet commonly does so.



\---



**# 19. AUTHORS AS DATA**



A new research idea:



Instead of only looking for quotations that fit an already selected archetype,

we can investigate whether the authors themselves provide independent data.



Potential relationship:



> \*\*Author → birth information → expressed perspective\*\*



For example:



If an authentic quotation expresses a particular perspective, and its author

independently belongs to a hypothesized Council position, that may be an

interesting observation.



However:



> \*\*This is evidence to investigate, not proof of the model.\*\*



\---



**# 20. AVOID CONFIRMATION BIAS**



We must not do:



1\. Decide that an author belongs to type X.

2\. Search their work.

3\. Select a quotation that confirms X.

4\. Declare success.



That would make the theory self-confirming.



A cleaner approach:



1\. Collect authentic quotations.

2\. Verify their authorship.

3\. Classify themes independently.

4\. Observe which people prefer which quotations.

5\. Compare the observations with the Council model.

6\. Only then investigate relationships with authors' birth information.



The preferred direction is:



> \*\*Voices → observations → patterns\*\*



not:



> \*\*Model → desired quote → confirmation\*\*



This is another application of the Elephant Principle.

**# 21. THREE TYPES OF QUOTATIONS**



The future quotation library may contain:



**## A — Historical quotations**



Authentic quotation from a historical person.



Author and source are provided.



**## B — Inspired quotations**



Newly written statements inspired by a philosophical idea.



They must never be falsely attributed.



**## C — Original Metal Monkey quotations**



Completely new statements created specifically for the project.



These may be written to have the same intellectual weight as historical

quotations.



The distinction must always remain clear.

The central research question is changing.



Instead of:



> "How do we make the test correctly identify the person?"

> \*\*"What do people naturally respond to when they are simply shown

This is a more open research question.

**# 24. THE TEST SHOULD NOT REQUIRE BELIEF**



The participant should not need to believe in:



\- astrology

\- personality types

\- the Chinese zodiac

\- the Council of 60

\- elements

\- archetypes



The test should still be interesting without belief.



A person can simply say:



> "I preferred this thought."



The system can then reveal a possible pattern.



The participant may accept it, reject it or remain curious.



All three reactions are valid.



\---



**# 25. THE MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLE**



> \*\*Do not tell people who they are.  

> Let them discover what naturally attracts them.\*\*



This may be the cleanest formulation of the new direction.



The test should function more like a mirror than a verdict.



\---



**# 26. PROJECT DEVELOPMENT PHILOSOPHY**



Metal Monkey itself must follow the principle it teaches.



If new evidence contradicts an existing hypothesis:



> \*\*Change the hypothesis before changing the evidence.\*\*



If another perspective explains the observations better:



> \*\*Move to the new perspective.\*\*



An earlier version of the test should never be considered embarrassing

because a later version improves it.



Every version records where we were standing around the elephant at that time.

**# 28. VERSIONING RULES**



Rule 1 — Never overwrite an old context version.



Old versions are historical records.



**Rule 2 — Every new version begins with a Change Log.**



Record:

* date
* previous version
* what changed
* why it changed
* what was learned
* what remains uncertain
* next step



**Rule 3 — Preserve rejected ideas.**



Do not delete a hypothesis merely because it later failed.



A rejected idea may become useful when viewed from another perspective.



**Rule 4 — Separate observation from interpretation.**



Example:



&#x09;OBSERVATION:

&#x09;Participant hesitated between Earth and Water.



&#x09;INTERPRETATION:

&#x09;The element statements may be too similar.



These are not the same thing.



**Rule 5 — Never silently convert hypotheses into facts.**



Use explicit status markers.





**# 29. STATUS MARKERS**



The following markers may be used throughout the project:



🟢 CONFIRMED



Repeatedly observed or strongly supported.



🟡 HYPOTHESIS



Interesting working explanation that requires testing.



🔵 EXPERIMENT



Currently being tested.



🔴 REJECTED



Currently abandoned because evidence contradicted it.



⚪ OPEN



Important question with insufficient evidence.



These markers are not permanent.



A future version may move an idea from one status to another.





**# 30. WHY THIS MATTERS**



Version control is not merely technical organization.



It protects the intellectual history of Metal Monkey.


A future version should be able to look backward and understand:



&#x09;What did we believe?

&#x09;Why did we believe it?

&#x09;What did we observe?

&#x09;What changed?

&#x09;Why did we change it?



This prevents the project from unconsciously rewriting its own history.





**# 31. METAL MONKEY MUST FOLLOW ITS OWN PRINCIPLE**



The project itself should behave like the Council.



Different versions may represent different perspectives.



One version may emphasize prediction.



Another may emphasize observation.



Another may discover that the test is actually a communication tool.



We do not need to erase the earlier perspective.



We need to understand what it could see.



&#x09;Every version is another place around the elephant.

The following questions remain open:

* Should quotations be used for animals, elements, trigons or all layers? 
* Are the three animal temptations sufficiently specific? 
* Can element choices be made more spontaneous? 
* Can historical quotations reveal meaningful patterns? 
* How much can author birth information contribute? 
* Can the Council improve communication between people with very different perspectives?

These questions should remain open until tested.





**# 33. NEXT EXPERIMENT**



Do not immediately rebuild the entire test.



The next practical task is:



> Build a library of strong, meaningful quotations.



Collect broadly before assigning them to Council positions.



For each quotation, preserve its source and attribution.



Then examine:



* what the quotation expresses 
* what values it contains 
* what kind of person might naturally be attracted to it 
* whether different people consistently prefer it 
* whether the author provides an interesting independent data point 



Only afterwards should quotations be assigned to test layers.

* a bridge between people who naturally struggle to understand one another 



The test is only one possible doorway into that system.
