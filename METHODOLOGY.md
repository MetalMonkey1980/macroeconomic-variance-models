# METAL MONKEY — METHODOLOGY

## 1. Purpose

METAL MONKEY is an exploratory framework.

Its methodology exists to prevent an important danger:

> **finding patterns first and inventing explanations afterward.**

The project therefore separates observation, interpretation, hypothesis, modelling and evidence.

The central methodological principle is:

> **The Metal Monkey is curious enough to see a pattern and disciplined enough to try to disprove it.**

---

## 2. The Evidence Ladder

METAL MONKEY should classify claims according to their evidential status.

### Level 0 — Idea

A possibility worth considering.

Example:

> "Birth-season conditions might influence later development."

No evidence is implied.

### Level 1 — Traditional Claim

A claim originating in a historical or cultural tradition.

Example:

> "The Chinese zodiac associates particular birth combinations with particular characteristics."

This documents tradition, not scientific proof.

### Level 2 — Observation

A repeatable observation that appears in collected data.

Example:

> "A particular group appears to contain a higher proportion of a measured characteristic."

### Level 3 — Correlation

A statistically measurable relationship between variables.

### Level 4 — Hypothesis

A proposed explanation that could potentially be tested.

### Level 5 — Supported Model

A hypothesis that survives multiple tests and competing explanations.

### Level 6 — Replicated Finding

A relationship reproduced independently using appropriate data and methods.

Even a replicated finding remains open to revision.

---

## 3. Separate the Layers

Every METAL MONKEY analysis should distinguish at least four layers:

### Tradition

What a historical system says.

### Interpretation

How METAL MONKEY maps that tradition into a modern conceptual model.

### Measurement

What can actually be observed and quantified.

### Explanation

What mechanism might account for the observed relationship.

These layers must not be silently substituted for one another.

For example:

> "The Fire element is traditionally associated with certain characteristics"

is different from:

> "People classified as Fire display a statistically measurable difference"

which is different again from:

> "The Fire classification causes that difference."

---

## 4. The Council of 60

The Council of 60 is based on the traditional combination of:

**12 animal/earthly-branch associations × 5 elemental phases**

producing a 60-position cycle.

METAL MONKEY treats these positions as candidate archetypes.

The computational model should allow each archetype to possess a defined set of characteristics.

Those characteristics should be:

* explicitly documented;
* versioned;
* open to revision;
* distinguishable from measured human traits.

The model must never silently convert symbolic descriptions into scientific facts.

---

## 5. Building the Archetypes

Each archetype should have a structured record containing, where applicable:

* name;
* traditional designation;
* cycle position;
* associated animal;
* associated elemental phase;
* traditional characteristics;
* METAL MONKEY interpretation;
* proposed behavioral dimensions;
* confidence level;
* evidence supporting each proposed dimension;
* contradictory evidence;
* revision history.

Example:

```text
Archetype:
Fire + Dragon

Traditional association:
[documented separately]

Proposed METAL MONKEY dimensions:
[explicitly defined]

Evidence:
[references / datasets]

Confidence:
[low / medium / high]

Status:
[hypothesis / tested / supported / rejected]
```

---

## 6. Avoiding Confirmation Bias

Because METAL MONKEY investigates patterns, confirmation bias is one of its greatest methodological risks.

Researchers should actively search for evidence that contradicts the hypothesis.

For every proposed relationship, ask:

1. What evidence would support this?
2. What evidence would contradict it?
3. What alternative explanation could produce the same result?
4. Could the result arise from sampling bias?
5. Could the result arise from cultural differences?
6. Could age, sex, geography, socioeconomic status or historical period explain it?
7. Could the pattern disappear in an independent dataset?

A hypothesis that cannot potentially be disproved is not a useful empirical hypothesis.

---

## 7. Statistical Testing

Where numerical data are available, METAL MONKEY should use appropriate statistical methods.

Depending on the question, these may include:

* descriptive statistics;
* correlation analysis;
* regression;
* classification;
* clustering;
* time-series analysis;
* survival analysis;
* Bayesian methods;
* permutation tests;
* cross-validation;
* sensitivity analysis;
* and out-of-sample testing.

The selected method must follow the question and data rather than being chosen to produce a desired result.

---

## 8. Confounding Variables

Human behaviour is influenced by many variables.

A zodiac-related observation may actually be explained by:

* geography;
* culture;
* family;
* education;
* socioeconomic status;
* generation;
* historical period;
* occupation;
* nutrition;
* health;
* climate;
* migration;
* selection effects;
* or other unknown variables.

Whenever possible, analyses should measure and control for relevant confounding factors.

---

## 9. Birth Conditions

If METAL MONKEY investigates birth conditions, datasets may include:

* birth date;
* birth season;
* latitude;
* temperature;
* daylight duration;
* humidity;
* environmental conditions;
* socioeconomic conditions;
* and later-life outcomes.

The project should avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

Where possible, anonymized or aggregated datasets should be preferred.

---

## 10. Environmental "Energy"

The word **energy** can have many meanings.

METAL MONKEY should avoid treating all uses of the word as equivalent.

A physical definition might refer to measurable quantities such as:

* temperature;
* solar radiation;
* electromagnetic radiation;
* gravitational effects;
* chemical energy;
* or other measurable physical variables.

A traditional definition may refer to a symbolic or philosophical concept.

These must be labelled separately.

The project may investigate whether a traditional concept corresponds to measurable phenomena, but it must not assume equivalence.

---

## 11. Astronomy and Planetary Cycles

METAL MONKEY may calculate astronomical cycles involving:

* Sun;
* Moon;
* Mercury;
* Venus;
* Mars;
* Jupiter;
* Saturn;
* Uranus;
* Neptune;
* and other relevant bodies.

The project should distinguish:

### Astronomical fact

For example:

> "Jupiter and Saturn have orbital periods of approximately X and Y."

### Derived cycle

For example:

> "Their relative configuration repeats approximately according to X."

### Historical correlation

For example:

> "A particular class of events appears more frequently near X."

### Causal hypothesis

For example:

> "The astronomical configuration contributes to a biological or psychological mechanism."

These are four different claims.

---

## 12. Planetary Influence

METAL MONKEY may investigate whether planetary configurations correlate with measurable human or environmental variables.

However:

> **Correlation must not be presented as proof of planetary causation.**

Any proposed mechanism should be independently examined.

Possible mechanisms must be physically plausible and quantitatively evaluated.

If no plausible mechanism can be identified, the relationship may still be investigated statistically, but the limitation must remain explicit.

---

## 13. Historical Cycles

Historical events may be compared with recurring cycles.

This can include:

* wars;
* political transitions;
* economic crises;
* technological changes;
* environmental events;
* demographic changes.

However, historical datasets are vulnerable to:

* selective reporting;
* survivorship bias;
* incomplete records;
* changing definitions;
* retrospective pattern matching;
* and arbitrary choice of start/end dates.

Therefore, historical-cycle analysis should be preregistered or otherwise defined before the results are examined whenever practical.

---

## 14. Conflict Analysis

METAL MONKEY may model conflict as an interaction between:

* individual leaders;
* institutions;
* states;
* economic incentives;
* resources;
* historical relationships;
* public opinion;
* security concerns;
* and environmental conditions.

The Council of 60 can be used as a perspective model.

It should not be used to claim that a leader will behave a particular way simply because of their birth classification.

For example:

> "Leader X is a Dragon, therefore war is likely"

is not an acceptable conclusion.

A valid analysis would instead examine measurable variables and, if desired, test whether the archetypal classification contributes statistically after controlling for other factors.

---

## 15. The Peace Dividend Model

METAL MONKEY may model the potential resources released by reducing conflict.

Possible components include:

```text
Peace Dividend =
avoided destructive expenditure
+ preserved infrastructure
+ recovered economic activity
+ recovered human productivity
+ avoided environmental damage
+ reduced reconstruction requirements
```

This is a modelling framework, not a claim that every military expenditure can be eliminated.

Models should provide:

* assumptions;
* ranges;
* uncertainty;
* alternative scenarios;
* sensitivity analysis.

Fixed formulas should never be treated as universal truths merely because they originated in an early METAL MONKEY concept.

---

## 16. Resource Transformation

A central METAL MONKEY question is:

> **What could existing resources become under different decisions?**

For example:

```text
Industrial capacity
        ↓
 ┌──────┴──────┐
 ↓             ↓
Destruction   Regeneration
 ↓             ↓
Weapons       Energy
Conflict      Water
Damage        Infrastructure
Loss          Restoration
```

The purpose is not to demonize technology or industry.

It is to investigate whether existing technological and industrial capabilities can produce greater long-term human and planetary resilience.

---

## 17. Simulation

METAL MONKEY simulations should clearly identify:

* inputs;
* assumptions;
* parameters;
* equations;
* initial conditions;
* random variables;
* outputs;
* uncertainty;
* and limitations.

A simulation is not evidence that its assumptions are true.

It is a tool for exploring consequences **conditional on those assumptions**.

The correct interpretation is:

> "If these assumptions are approximately correct, this is what the model predicts."

Not:

> "The simulation proves that this will happen."

---

## 18. The Council as a Decision Model

The Council of 60 can eventually become a computational decision-support system.

A proposed decision could be evaluated through 60 archetypal perspectives.

For example:

```text
Decision
   ↓
60 perspectives
   ↓
Potential benefits
Potential risks
Potential objections
Potential unintended consequences
   ↓
Comparison
   ↓
Human decision
```

The Council does not vote on reality.

It exposes perspectives that might otherwise be ignored.

---

## 19. Multi-Perspective Robustness

A useful future metric could be **Council Robustness**.

A proposed decision would be considered more robust when it remains acceptable or defensible across a larger number of independent perspectives.

This does not mean majority rule.

A minority perspective may identify a catastrophic risk that a majority overlooks.

Therefore the model should record:

* majority support;
* minority objections;
* extreme-risk objections;
* unresolved conflicts;
* and uncertainty.

---

## 20. Reproducibility

Important analyses should be reproducible.

Where possible, the project should publish:

* source code;
* methodology;
* data sources;
* processing steps;
* model parameters;
* version information;
* and results.

Private or legally restricted datasets may require alternative procedures, but the methodology should remain as transparent as possible.

---

## 21. Versioning and Historical Integrity

METAL MONKEY should preserve a history of important methodological changes.

When a model changes, the project should document:

* what changed;
* why it changed;
* what evidence motivated the change;
* and whether previous results changed.

The goal is not to create an appearance of perfection.

The goal is to make the evolution of knowledge visible.

---

## 22. Independent Challenge

Important conclusions should be exposed to criticism.

The project should actively welcome:

* replication;
* alternative models;
* contradictory datasets;
* methodological criticism;
* statistical criticism;
* philosophical criticism;
* and criticism of the project's assumptions.

A successful challenge is not an attack on METAL MONKEY.

It is part of the methodology.

---

## 23. AI-Assisted Research

Artificial intelligence may be used for:

* data exploration;
* coding assistance;
* literature organization;
* hypothesis generation;
* simulation;
* classification;
* summarization;
* and alternative-perspective generation.

AI-generated results must be independently checked where accuracy matters.

AI should not be treated as an oracle.

The same principle applies to METAL MONKEY itself:

> **A sophisticated model can still be wrong.**

---

The intended workflow is:

```text

     ↓

     ↓

     ↓

     ↓
```

The process may return to an earlier stage at any point.

---

## 25. The Final Methodological Principle

METAL MONKEY exists to discover whether interesting ideas survive examination.

It should never protect a beautiful idea from an inconvenient result.

The Monkey says:

> **"Look at this pattern."**

The Metal says:

> **"Prove it."**

The Council says:

> **"Show us what we are missing."**

The evidence says:

> **"Now decide what remains."**

And the final authority remains outside the model:

> **Human beings must decide what to do with what they learn.**
