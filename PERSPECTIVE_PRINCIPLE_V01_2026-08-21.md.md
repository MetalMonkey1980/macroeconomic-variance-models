**# Metal Monkey — The Perspective Principle**



> ### You are not wrong.

> ### You are standing somewhere.



\*\*Version:\*\* V01  

\*\*Date:\*\* 2026-08-21  

\*\*Status:\*\* Working philosophical document  

\*\*Related:\*\* Council of 60 / Elephant Principle / Personality Test



\---



**## 1. The Problem**



Many people spend a significant part of their lives believing that

something is wrong with them.



They may feel that they are too slow, too emotional, too quiet,

too ambitious, too sensitive, too stubborn, too different,

or simply incapable of doing things the way other people expect.



But what if the problem is not the person?



What if the person is simply looking at the world from a different place?



\---



**## 2. The Elephant**



A person standing around a large elephant cannot see the whole elephant.



They can see a part of it.



Another person standing somewhere else sees another part.



Both descriptions may be completely honest.



Neither person is necessarily lying.



Neither person necessarily lacks intelligence.



They simply have different perspectives.



The conflict begins when one person assumes that their perspective

is the entire elephant.



\---



**## 3. The Same Is True of People**



Human beings do not experience the world from an objective,

identical position.



We are shaped by:



\- our natural disposition,

\- our experiences,

\- our environment,

\- our relationships,

\- our culture,

\- our education,

\- our successes,

\- our failures,

\- and the perspectives we have encountered throughout life.



Two people can experience the same event and honestly reach

completely different conclusions.



This does not automatically mean that one of them is wrong.



They may simply be standing somewhere else.



\---



**## 4. The Shoes**



There is a familiar idea expressed in many forms:



> \*\*"You cannot judge my walk if you have not walked in my shoes."\*\*



The exact wording and origin may vary.



The principle is more important than the quotation:



\*\*A person's behavior cannot always be understood without understanding

the place from which that person is experiencing the world.\*\*



\---



**## 5. Living Someone Else's Life**



A person can spend years trying to satisfy expectations created

by people standing somewhere else around the elephant.



Parents may expect one thing.



Partners another.



Society another.



Colleagues another.



The person may gradually learn to distrust their own perception.



They begin to ask:



> "Why can't I be like them?"



Instead, perhaps they should sometimes ask:



> \*\*"Why am I standing here?"\*\*



Understanding one's own perspective can bring relief.



Not because it proves that everything one does is correct.



But because it explains why certain things feel natural,

while other things constantly feel forced.



\---



**## 6. The Mirror**



This changes the purpose of a personality test.



A personality test does not necessarily reveal an eternal,

unchanging identity.



It may reflect the way a person currently sees the world.



The result can therefore change.



And that does not automatically make the test meaningless.



It may mean that the mirror is reflecting something different.



\---



**## 7. A Personal Example**



A person may take a personality test many years ago

and receive one result.



Later, after significant life experiences, education,

self-reflection or a profound change in worldview,

the same person may receive another result.



The first result does not necessarily have to be discarded.



The second result does not necessarily have to replace it.



Both may be records of the person's perspective

at different moments in life.



The test may have reflected the journey.



\---



**## 8. The Metal Monkey Principle**



The purpose of the Metal Monkey personality test

is therefore not to declare:



> \*\*"This is who you are."\*\*



It is to invite the person to notice:



> \*\*"This is what I naturally see."\*\*



That distinction is fundamental.



The test should not become another authority

telling people how they should behave.



It should become a mirror that helps them observe themselves.



\---



**## 9. Knowing Yourself Without Rejecting Others**



Understanding one's own perspective does not mean

that other perspectives become wrong.



Quite the opposite.



The more clearly a person understands where they stand,

the easier it becomes to recognize that others are standing

somewhere else.



This creates the possibility of respect without agreement.



Understanding without imitation.



Cooperation without sameness.



\---



**## 10. The Council**



The Council of 60 is built on this principle.



Sixty perspectives do not exist because sixty people

must eventually agree.



They exist because no single perspective can see the entire elephant.



The Council is therefore not a machine for producing consensus.



It is a structure for making the invisible visible.



\---



**## 11. The Test as a Seed**



The greatest success of the test may not be correctly predicting

a person's birth year.



The greater success may occur when a person finishes the test

and begins to wonder:



> "Why did I choose that answer?"



And then:



> "Why did they choose a different one?"



And finally:



> \*\*"Maybe neither of us is wrong."\*\*



That question is the seed.



\---



**## 12. The Principle**



> \*\*You are not wrong.

> You are standing somewhere.\*\*



And:



> \*\*You cannot expect someone to see what you see

> if you have never shown them where you are standing.\*\*



And finally:



> \*\*Never delete a perspective just because you found a better one.\*\*



\---



**## 13. What Metal Monkey Is Trying to Do**



Metal Monkey does not attempt to tell people

which perspective is the correct one.



It attempts to make perspectives visible.



Because once a person can see their own position,

and recognize that others occupy different positions,

a conflict can change its nature.



The question is no longer:



> \*\*"Who is right?"\*\*



It becomes:



> \*\*"What can you see that I cannot?"\*\*



And that is where the Council begins.

No single person sees the whole elephant.



That is not a failure.



That is the reason we need one another.



\*\*Metal Monkey Project\*\*



\*Keep looking. Keep listening.\*



\*The elephant is bigger than any of us.\*





**## 14. The Bridges Between Perspectives**



One person cannot see the whole elephant.



That is why we need another person.



But there is another problem:



The second person may see the elephant from such a different

position that the two people cannot understand one another.



This is not necessarily a failure either.



We do not need every person to understand every other person directly.



Sometimes we need a third person who can understand both.



And sometimes a fourth, fifth or tenth.



A person who can translate one perspective into another

becomes a bridge between two worlds.



The Council therefore does not require everyone

to speak the same language.



It requires enough connections for different languages

to eventually reach one another.



Perhaps the greatest value of a person is not what they see alone.



Perhaps it is also \*\*who they can understand.\*\*



\---



**### The Council**



The Council of 60 is therefore not simply a collection

of sixty different perspectives.



It is a network of connections between them.



Some people will naturally understand one another.



Some will not.



Some will stand almost opposite each other.



That is acceptable.



There may be someone between them who understands both.



The goal is not to make every person compatible with every other person.



The goal is to create enough bridges

that no perspective becomes isolated.



> \*\*You do not have to understand everyone.

> You only need to know who can help you understand them.\*\*





**## 15. The Natural Bridges**



The Council of 60 is not a collection of sixty isolated personalities.



If every person were completely separated from everyone else,

the Council would have little meaning.



The deeper idea is that every person is connected to others

through different layers of their nature.



The four layers of the model can therefore be understood

not only as filters for identifying a person,

but also as different kinds of bridges between people.



\---



**### The Four Bridges**



**#### 1. The Trigon — Shared Foundation**



The twelve animals form four natural groups.



People within the same trigon may have a deeper common foundation,

even when their individual personalities are very different.



A person may therefore understand another person

not because they are alike,

but because they share something fundamental.



\---



**#### 2. The Inner Trigon — Secret, Spotlight and Challenge**



Within each trigon, different personalities respond

to different forms of attraction.



Some are drawn toward secrecy.



Some toward recognition and the spotlight.



Some toward challenge, risk and overcoming limits.



These differences can separate people,

but they can also create bridges.



A person does not need to react to the same temptation

to understand why another person reacts to it.



Understanding the difference is itself a connection.



\---



**#### 3. Yin / Yang — The Direction of Energy**



Yin and Yang describe another kind of connection.



Two people may have very different motivations,

yet respond to the outside world in a similar way.



Others may have similar goals,

but approach the world from opposite directions.



This creates another possible bridge between people:



\*\*not what they want, but how they meet the world.\*\*



\---



**#### 4. The Five Elements — The Deeper Motivation**



The elements reveal another possible connection:



\*\*What gives this person energy?\*\*



Two people may behave very differently,

yet be driven by a similar underlying need.



One may build.



Another may inspire.



Another may organize.



Another may understand.



Their actions may look different,

but somewhere underneath them there may be common ground.



\---



**## 16. A Person Has More Than One Connection**



This means that a person's identity is not simply a single point

inside a system of sixty types.



It is more like a position in a network.



A person may be connected to one person through their trigon,

to another through Yin / Yang,

to another through an element,

and to another through the way they respond to a challenge.



These connections do not need to point to the same people.



In fact, they probably should not.



The richness of the Council comes from the fact

that different layers create different paths between people.



\---



**## 17. The Bridges Matter More Than the Distance**



Two people may stand on opposite sides of the elephant.



They may have almost nothing in common at first sight.



They may even struggle to understand one another.



This does not mean that they are incompatible.



There may simply be no direct bridge between their perspectives.



But perhaps there is another person who understands one of them

and also understands the other.



That person becomes a bridge.



And perhaps there is another bridge beyond them.



The connection can therefore continue through the Council.



\*\*A person does not have to understand everyone directly.\*\*



They only need to be connected to someone who can help them

reach another perspective.



\---



**## 18. The Council as a Network**



The Council of 60 can therefore be imagined not as a circle

of sixty isolated positions,



but as a network of connections.



The animals are the positions.



The four layers create the bridges.



The people create the living connections.



Some bridges will be strong.



Some will be weak.



Some people will naturally understand one another.



Others may never fully understand each other.



That is not a failure of the system.



It is a natural consequence of perspective.



The purpose of the Council is not to remove the distance

between people.



It is to make the bridges across that distance visible.



\---



**## 19. From Classification to Connection**



This changes the purpose of the personality test once again.



The test does not have to end with:



> \*\*"This is your type."\*\*



It can continue with:



> \*\*"These are the perspectives you may naturally understand."\*\*



And perhaps even more importantly:



> \*\*"These are the perspectives that may be difficult for you to understand."\*\*



The second question may be more valuable than the first.



Because the people who are hardest for us to understand

may be the people who show us the parts of the elephant

that we cannot see.



\---



**## 🐘 The Council Principle**



> \*\*Sixty perspectives do not create sixty walls.  

> They create the possibility of thousands of bridges.\*\*



The purpose of the Council is not to make everyone alike.



It is to discover the connections that already exist

between people who are different.



Because sometimes the person who understands you

is not the person who is most like you.



Sometimes they are the person who stands somewhere else

and can still see what you see.



\---



**> \*\*The Council does not eliminate distance between perspectives.**

**> It builds bridges across it.\*\***
