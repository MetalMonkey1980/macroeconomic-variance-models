**# Metal Monkey — Project Context**



> \*\*The Council does not exist to find the person who is right.  

> It exists so that the elephant does not remain invisible.\*\*



This document preserves the current conceptual state of the Metal Monkey project.



It is not a final theory and it is not a scientific claim.

It is a working context: what we are trying to discover, what we have learned,

what we currently believe is useful, and what remains uncertain.



\---



**## 1. The Core Idea**



The project began with a simple observation:



People can look at the same reality and see very different things.



This does not necessarily mean that one person is right and another is wrong.

They may simply be observing reality from different positions.



The classic metaphor is the elephant:



Imagine twelve people standing around a large elephant.



One sees a leg.

Another sees the trunk.

Another sees the tail.

Another sees an ear.



They begin to argue about what the elephant really is.



But the problem is not that one of them is necessarily lying.



Each person is describing something real.



The problem is that each person can mistake their partial view for the whole elephant.



\### The Elephant Principle



> \*\*The Council does not exist to find the person who is right.  

> It exists so that the elephant does not remain invisible.\*\*



The purpose of the Council of 60 is therefore not to eliminate differences.



It is to make different perspectives visible and allow them to complement one another.



\---



**## 2. Council of 60**



The Council of 60 is a conceptual framework built around 60 possible

perspectives.



The traditional Chinese zodiac provides the initial structural inspiration:

12 animals combined with 5 elements produce 60 combinations.



However, the project should not be reduced to "Chinese astrology".



The more interesting question is:



> Can the 60 traditional combinations be interpreted as a structured

> set of different human perspectives?



The project does not assume that this is true.



It is a hypothesis to be explored.



The goal is not to prove that a person's birth date determines their personality.



The goal is to investigate whether the 60 positions can serve as a useful

map of different ways of observing, interpreting and approaching reality.



\---



**## 3. Perspective, Not Personality Prison**



The Council should never tell a person:



> "This is who you are."



A better formulation is:



> "This may be the perspective from which you naturally tend to look."



A perspective is not a complete identity.



A person is also shaped by:



\- life experience

\- family

\- culture

\- education

\- social environment

\- trauma and success

\- relationships

\- historical period

\- current circumstances

\- conscious decisions



Therefore observed behaviour is not necessarily identical to a person's

underlying or natural preference.



The project should not attempt to "clean" a person from all life experience.



Instead, it should explore what remains naturally attractive or intuitive

when a person is invited to observe their own reactions.



\---



**## 4. The Test — Its Real Purpose**



The original goal of the test was ambitious:



> Identify one of 60 types using only four tasks.



The four layers currently explored are:



1\. Yin / Yang

2\. Trigon

3\. Animal

4\. Element



The exact construction is still experimental.



We have NOT yet established that four tasks can reliably identify all 60

positions.



This must remain an open research question.



\### An important change of perspective



At first, the test was evaluated mainly by asking:



> "Did it correctly identify the person's birth year?"



We now consider this too narrow.



A successful test does not necessarily have to astonish a person by correctly

guessing their birth year.



It may be successful if it causes them to notice:



> "Someone else answered the same situation differently from me."



That moment can be more valuable than an accurate prediction.



\---



**## 5. The Seed of Perspective**



The test can act as a seed.



Two people are given the same situation.



They choose different answers.



Neither answer is necessarily wrong.



One person may suddenly wonder:



> "Why did we see the same thing so differently?"



That question is the seed.



It moves a person:



\- from judgment to observation

\- from certainty to curiosity

\- from "I am right" to "I wonder what you see"

\- from defending a position to exploring another perspective



\### The first seed



Before a person can understand the Council of 60,

they must first discover that their own perspective is not universal.



They do not need to believe in the system.



They do not need to accept an archetype.



They do not even need to agree with the result.



They only need to notice:



> \*\*"Someone else looked at the same thing — and saw something different."\*\*



The seed has been planted.



\---



**## 6. Observation Mode vs Decision Mode**



A key insight from the test development is the distinction between two

mental modes.



\### Decision mode



The person asks:



\- What is correct?

\- What should I do?

\- What should others do?

\- How do I defend my position?

\- What would society expect?

\- How do I win?



This mode naturally creates competition between perspectives.



\### Observation mode



The person asks:



\- What attracted me first?

\- What did I notice?

\- What felt natural?

\- Why did I choose this?

\- What might another person see differently?

\- What am I not seeing?



The test should attempt to activate the second mode.



The person should not feel that they are solving a personality puzzle.



They should feel that they are observing their own spontaneous reactions.



\---



**## 7. The Test Must Not Become a Social Desirability Test**



A critical design principle:



Every answer must be attractive to somebody.



No answer should be deliberately stupid, weak, immoral, ridiculous,

cowardly or humiliating.



The person should be able to think:



> "This one is definitely me."



while also being able to think:



> "That one isn't me, but I understand why somebody else might choose it."



This is essential.



The test must create \*\*difference without contempt\*\*.



\### Design rule



> \*\*Choose what feels most like you.  

> Do not judge what feels most like someone else.\*\*



The answers should therefore be:



\- clearly differentiated

\- psychologically meaningful

\- attractive to different perspectives

\- neutral in moral status

\- difficult to rank as objectively "better"



The goal is not to create winners and losers.



The goal is to reveal differences.



\---



**## 8. Why Early Test Failures Were Valuable**



Initial experiments did not reliably reveal the participant's actual birth year.



One example produced:



\- predicted: Wood + Yin + Rabbit

\- actual: Wood + Yin + Goat



This initially appeared to be a failure.



However, it revealed something important.



The participant may have been attracted by one temptation even though another

motivation might have been deeper.



This raised an important distinction:



> \*\*What attracts a person in a situation is not necessarily identical to

> their complete personality.\*\*



It also showed that life experience and learned behaviour may influence

answers.



Instead of immediately "fixing" the result, we should use such cases to

understand what the test is actually measuring.



A failed prediction is therefore data.



\---



**## 9. The Current Philosophy of Test Development**



We originally considered trying to remove the influence of life experience,

social media, upbringing and social expectations.



We no longer consider this necessarily the right goal.



The more useful objective is:



> Create choices that are sufficiently distinct that a person naturally

> chooses the one closest to them, while respecting the other choices.



We do not need to perfectly isolate an imaginary "pure personality".



We need to create a useful mirror of perspective.



The test should reveal difference rather than claim absolute identity.



\---



**## 10. The Four-Layer Test**



The current experimental structure contains four layers:



\### Layer 1 — Yin / Yang



A binary distinction concerning the way energy is directed and how a person

responds to the outside world.



\### Layer 2 — Trigon



A broader grouping of related perspectives.



\### Layer 3 — Animal



A more specific archetypal perspective within the trigon.



\### Layer 4 — Element



A further dimension describing the dominant style of energy:



\- Wood — growth and development

\- Fire — passion and intensity

\- Earth — stability and tangible results

\- Metal — order and justice

\- Water — intuition, communication and adaptability



These descriptions are working interpretations, not established scientific facts.



The exact mapping and test architecture remain under development.



\---



**## 11. What We Are NOT Trying to Do**



The project should NOT become:



\- a horoscope that tells people what they must be

\- a hierarchy of superior and inferior personalities

\- a way to prove that one perspective is objectively correct

\- a system that excuses harmful behaviour

\- a system that encourages people to stereotype others

\- a test where one answer is obviously more socially desirable

\- a mechanism for forcing people into fixed identities



The Council should increase curiosity, not reduce people to labels.



\---



**## 12. The Deeper Purpose**



A person involved in a conflict may think:



> "I am right. They are wrong."



The Council asks a different question:



> "What are you seeing that they cannot see?"



And then:



> "What might they be seeing that you cannot?"



This changes the nature of disagreement.



The purpose is not to make two radically different people become identical.



It is to create communication paths between different perspectives.



A person may naturally understand some perspectives better than others.



These natural connections can potentially form bridges between people who would

otherwise struggle to communicate.



The Council therefore represents not just 60 personalities,

but potentially a \*\*network of perspectives\*\*.



\---



**## 13. The Elephant**



The ultimate metaphor of the project is not the 60 archetypes.



It is the elephant.



The 60 positions are different places from which the elephant may be observed.



No single position should be assumed to contain the whole picture.



The objective is not:



> "Which person sees the elephant correctly?"



The objective is:



> \*\*"How can we combine enough perspectives that the elephant becomes visible?"\*\*



\### Final principle



> \*\*A perspective is not the whole truth.  

> It is a position from which a part of the truth becomes visible.\*\*



And:



> \*\*The Council does not exist to find the person who is right.  

> It exists so that the elephant does not remain invisible.\*\*



\---



**## 14. Current Research Status**



The Council of 60 remains a hypothesis-driven project.



The following are currently exploratory:



\- whether the 60 archetypes correspond to reproducible behavioural patterns

\- whether the four-layer test can distinguish the 60 positions

\- whether birth-year information can be predicted above chance

\- how strongly life experience affects the responses

\- whether different people consistently show different perspectives

\- whether the framework can improve communication between different

&#x20; perspectives



No conclusion should be treated as established until it survives systematic

testing.



The project should preserve failures and unexpected results.



Do not modify the model simply to make an individual test result fit.



\---



**## 15. The Next Step**



The next task is not to add more concepts.



It is to build and test the four-layer experiment carefully.



For each participant, record:



\- answers

\- predicted profile

\- actual birth information

\- observed personality / perspective where independently known

\- agreement or disagreement

\- unexpected behaviour

\- participant's own reaction to the result



Most importantly:



\*\*Do not judge the test only by whether it predicts the birth year.\*\*



Also record whether the test creates the intended effect:



> Did the person notice that another person sees the same situation differently?



> Did they become curious rather than judgmental?



> Did they begin to observe their own reactions?



> Did the result make them consider that their perspective might not be the

> whole picture?



This may be the first measurable sign that the Council is doing what it was

designed to do.



\---



**## 16. Working Motto**



> \*\*We do not need to make everyone see the same elephant.\*\*



> \*\*We need to help everyone realize that there is an elephant.\*\*
