**# The Seed of Perspective**



The purpose of the Council is not to tell people who they are.



It is to make them notice that other people may see the same world differently.



A test does not have to correctly identify a person's archetype to be valuable.



Sometimes the most important result is much simpler:



Two people are presented with the same situation.

They choose different answers.

Neither answer is wrong.



And one of them asks:



> "Why did we see the same thing so differently?"



That question is the seed.



It moves a person from judgment to observation.

From "I am right" to "I wonder why you see it differently."

From defending a position to becoming curious about another perspective.



The goal is not to erase differences.

The goal is to make differences visible without turning them into conflict.



\### The first seed



Before a person can understand the Council of 60,

they must first discover that their own perspective is not universal.



They do not need to believe in the system.

They do not need to accept an archetype.

They do not even need to agree with the result.



They only need to notice:



> \*\*"Someone else looked at the same thing — and saw something different."\*\*



That moment is enough.



The seed has been planted.



And once curiosity replaces certainty,

the person may become willing to look around the elephant.



The test does not need to change the person's mind.

It only needs to make the person curious about their own mind. 



The test is not designed to eliminate differences.

It is designed to make differences visible without making them hostile.



Choose what feels most like you.

Do not judge what feels most like someone else.
