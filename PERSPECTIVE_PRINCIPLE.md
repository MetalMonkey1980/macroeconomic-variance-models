**>>The Perspective Principle**



We do not seek to determine who is right.

We seek to discover what each perspective can see.



A human being rarely observes the whole system. Each person sees reality from a particular position, shaped by their character, experience, environment and way of processing information.



Conflict often begins when a partial perspective is mistaken for the complete truth.



The purpose of the Council of 60 is therefore not to determine which perspective is superior. It is to make different perspectives visible and allow them to complement one another.



The goal is not to choose who sees the elephant correctly.

The goal is to see the elephant.



METAL MONKEY does not ask:

“Who is right?”



It asks:

“What can you see that I cannot?”



**>>The Elephant Principle**



Imagine twelve people standing around a large elephant.

One sees a leg. Another sees the trunk. Another sees the tail. Another sees the ear.



They begin to argue about what the elephant really is.



The goal is not to decide which of them is right.



Each of them is describing something real — but none of them can see the whole elephant from their position.



The goal is to combine their perspectives until the whole elephant becomes visible.



This is the idea behind the Council of 60.



We do not need sixty people who think alike.

We need sixty different perspectives that can communicate with one another.



A perspective is not the whole truth.

It is a position from which a part of the truth becomes visible.



When two people see different parts of the elephant, the answer is not to make one of them blind — it is to help them see what the other one sees. 



The Council does not exist to find the person who is right.

It exists so that the elephant does not remain invisible. 



“What part of the elephant can you see?”
